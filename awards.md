    <em>As team lead, developed an integrated statistical framework for macro-level attribution and scenario projection of carbon emissions from China's steel industry, combining a two-way fixed-effects STIRPAT-EKC model, Kaya-LMDI decomposition, CatBoost-SHAP nonlinear validation, and a Tiny Time Mixer (TTM) pretrained time-series model based on 2000–2023 panel data across five major steel-producing countries. Key results indicate that crude steel capacity is the dominant positive driver of emission growth (elasticity 1.411, significant at the 1% level, robust to instrument-variable estimation at 1.268–1.411); the Kaya-LMDI decomposition attributes an accumulated increment of 1,074.21 Mt of CO₂ over 2000–2023 to capacity-scale effects (+1,342.72 Mt), partially offset by emission-intensity improvements (−268.51 Mt); and the TTM model achieves a goodness of fit of 0.97, suggesting a rebound risk for China's steel emissions after peaking in 2028 in the absence of stronger policy support.</em><br>
